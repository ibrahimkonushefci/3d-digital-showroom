- **SCHMIDT:** inspected kitchen assets identified Hapticmedia/Apviz/Babylon, furnished scene modules, second-UV lighting maps, detailed material controls, a studio environment and a bounded 256 px local reflection probe. Finish changes queued reflection updates; mobile texture variants existed. Runtime inspection showed finish switching within furnished rooms and convincing wardrobe garments. We wanted its coordinated room presentation, focal models, material response, lighting depth and reflections. This was the closest architectural reference for our editable live 3D showroom. The original evidence did not establish its full authoring pipeline or real-time global illumination.
- **Cosentino:** the tested Onfigr kitchen declared `pano_cubic`; code/configuration showed six cube faces, per-surface JPEG tiles, layered material variants and texture blending. Its photographic appearance relies heavily on prepared imagery. We wanted its material presentation, composition and finish-zone ideas, while recognizing that arbitrary furniture layouts and opening drawers require a different approach. We did not plan to replace our editable designer with a panorama, and should not claim equivalent realism merely from adding lights.
- **IKEA PAX:** inspected IPEX bridge code connected product entities to model loading, transforms, shadows, picking and readiness. Browser testing showed individual shelves, drawers, rails and baskets, product quantities and price updates. We wanted separately editable wardrobe components, understandable selection and fit validation, and coherent pricing. The earlier analysis did not establish IKEA's exact manufacturing rules, model assets or complete lighting implementation. These usability improvements do not automatically produce a major visual upgrade.

Our existing renderer already had React Three Fiber/Three.js, procedural cabinets, PBR maps, environment reflections, shadows, LEDs, tone mapping and adaptive resolution. The identified gaps were sparse surroundings, simplified appliances/clothing, stretched panel textures, generic lighting/reflections, shallow-looking interiors, global finishes and whole-module storage configuration. The research supported improving content and lighting rather than changing engines by default.

The resulting sequence was: (1) physical material scale/construction, (2) furnished rooms/focal models, (3) lighting/reflections/interior depth, (4) independent finish zones, (5) individual wardrobe storage, and (6) cameras/touch/sales-flow/offline validation. Phase 0 covered baselines and contracts. The intended result was furnished surroundings around the customer's actual editable design, with visual quality visibly improved.
